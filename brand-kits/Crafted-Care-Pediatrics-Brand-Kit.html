Handmade medicine for growing families.

Krystyna Wesp, DO crochets hats for her newborn patients and hands out books in clinic. That is the practice. The identity only has to be honest about it.

Most pediatric branding reaches for the same shelf: primary colors, cartoon animals, balloons, a crayon wordmark. It reads as a waiting room, and it tells a parent nothing about the doctor. Crafted Care goes the other direction. The palette is sage and coral on a warm off-white. The mark is a parent holding a child, drawn in one continuous line, resting on an open book. The handmade part of this brand is real, so it leads the visual system instead of decorating it.

The audience is the parent, not the child. Usually a tired mother or father who has spent seven minutes with a pediatrician who was already late. Every choice below is aimed at that person.

Handmade

Books, yarn, hats for newborns. The craft is the differentiator, and it carries through to the stitch patterns used as edges and borders.

Unhurried

Her word, and better than anything we would write. Generous spacing, long line lengths, and copy that never rushes.

Whole child

Birth through 21, including behavioral health, newborn care, osteopathic manipulative medicine, and home visits for the first two months.

Warm and specific, never cute.

Warmth here comes from precision. Naming the crochet hats does more for a parent than any amount of soft language about caring deeply.

Sage and coral do the color. Cream and sage mist do the quiet backgrounds. Coral stays rare on purpose, so that when it shows up it means something.

Text rules. Sage and Coral are fill colors and never carry text, because neither reaches 2.5:1 on any ground in this system. For type on paper use Deep Sage at 6.41:1, Ink at 13.22:1, or Deep Coral at 5.71:1. Reversed, use Paper on Deep Sage at 6.41:1 or Paper on Deep Coral at 5.71:1. On a coral fill, use Ink at 5.46:1. Every pairing in this kit clears WCAG AA for body text.

Two typefaces, one with a voice.

One typeface has a voice and one stays out of the way. The Crafted Care wordmark follows the regular Literata serif direction, with no cursive or script treatment anywhere in the system.

Literata

Display. Headlines, section titles, pull quotes, the tagline. Weights 400, 500, 600.

Google had this drawn as the reading typeface for Play Books, so it is quite literally a face made for books. It carries headlines, the tagline, and anything meant to sound like Dr. Wesp talking.

How Crafted Care talks.

Warmth, comfort, and personal connection, in Dr. Wesp's words. Below is how that turns into rules the writing holds itself to.

Your pediatrician has time for the question you were going to ask in the parking lot.

Names the specific failure of a rushed visit without attacking anyone.

The first two months, we come to you.

Home visits from birth to two months are a genuine standout. Say it flatly and let it land.

Every newborn goes home with a hat I made. Every visit ends with a book.

First person from Dr. Wesp. A doctor talking to a parent, rather than a brand talking to a market.

We vaccinate on schedule, and we will talk it through as long as you need.

Confident about training and about vaccines, without arguing.

What it sounds like

Calm and specific. Short sentences and real numbers. A doctor talking to a parent. Comfortable naming boundaries, because being reachable is different from being on call around the clock.

What it never does

Baby talk, so no "little ones", no "tiny humans", no "mamas". No cartoon animals, balloons, or crayon lettering. No stock photography, because families here recognize a stock family. No pressure, so nothing like "spots are filling fast" on a waitlist for infants.

Never write 24/7

Also never around the clock, always available, or anytime access. Dr. Wesp is a mother and a patient before she is a 24 hour physician. Write direct access, same-day and next-day availability.

The call to action changes at launch

Until the December opening, every call to action is join the waitlist. On opening day it becomes schedule a meet-and-greet, sitewide and across social.

The mark.

A parent and child drawn as one continuous line, resting on an open book. Use the primary lockup whenever space allows, and keep the standalone mark for compact applications.

Primary lockup. Deep Sage on Paper.

Reversed. Paper on Deep Sage.

Mark alone. Use where the name is already present.

Mark on Coral. Ink, for social tiles and covers.

Wordmark alone. Email signatures, footers, tight horizontal spaces.

Wordmark on Paper.

64, 32 and 24px. The drawn mark holds down to 24px and closes up below that, because the line weight is finer than one pixel at 16px. A simplified single-weight favicon still needs to be drawn.

Clear space. Keep a margin equal to the height of the child's head on all four sides of the lockup, and never place the mark on a photograph or a pattern. Minimum size. 120px wide for the full lockup, 24px for the mark alone. Do not recolor the mark outside this palette, add a drop shadow, stretch it, or set the wordmark in any face other than the drawn original.

About this page

This is a portfolio view of the Crafted Care Pediatrics brand kit. Krystyna Wesp approved the logo on 26 August 2026, and the full kit was delivered on 27 August with a revised edition on 31 August. The colors, type pairing, and voice rules on this page are the approved ones.

The delivered kit runs wider than this page does. It also documents the two crochet stitch patterns used as edges and borders, the card and social applications, and the take-home pieces: a book plate for the inside cover of every book given out, a hat tag carrying the tagline and nothing else, and a membership card for each child.
